Presentation turns authoritative facts into pictures, sound, and feel. It never decides them (PRES-001).

> **Physics computes the fight once.** 3D animation, combat feel, readability and accessibility never reinterpret that fight; they expose the same solved state through additional spatial and sensory projections.

## Drawn swords meet where the plane says they touch (CONTACT-PRESENTATION-001)

The combat plane owns every contact's XY, each sword's yaw and each hilt's horizontal place; presentation adds a height. The snapshot carries every touching contact point read-only — where, which feature of each sword and where on it, its normal, its solved impulses, how fast it slides, how long it has lasted and its stable id — and no presentation code computes a contact of its own. A pitched blade's ground projection is shorter than the blade, so a sword can pass through the authoritative contact only while it is level there. While a contact exists each contacted sword is drawn level, hilt and yaw unchanged, both swords at one shared height (the mean of their free heights at the contact); they meet edge to edge exactly as the plane says, so there is no over/under to decide. The swords snap to that the frame contact begins; the arms follow through the IK, and on release the lift eases back. The clash sparks, the clang and the grind all sit at that same rendered contact (`ContactPresentation`).

## Readability is a projection of the physics (COMBAT-READABILITY-001)

Every gameplay-critical cue reads solved quantities — normal impulse, tangential impulse and slip, dissipated energy, blade velocity, contact age — and is carried on at least two senses: a clash is seen and heard; a body hit is marked on the victim and heard; a true whiff is heard as its own hollow follow-through (louder for a harder launch) and seen in the body's overswing, and never punctuated like a hit; sustained contact grinds in sparks and sound. Haptics are optional and supplemental. Colour never carries combat meaning alone.

Two optional channels read the same sources rather than adding their own. **Sound captions** (`SoundCaptions`, off by default) are picked from the very audio requests the mixer plays, so a caption can never disagree with the sound: at most one per tick, by a fixed order that keeps the physical hierarchy in words (a tap, a clash, a heavy clash, a hit, a critical), with a cut's lead take naming it and decorative layers, swings, footwork and the charge's tension left uncaptioned; a scrape is captioned once, when a contact first becomes audible. They have their own HUD line, so a clash never overwrites the announcer. The **contact highlight** (`ContactHighlight3D`, off by default) is a dark ring around a light one — a shape, not a colour — on every touching point of the snapshot's contact set, at the shared rendered contact height and drawn over the blades; it is never built when off, so it costs nothing.

Accessibility settings (combat FX intensity, flash, shake, motion, trails, high-contrast swords, haptics, announcer and sound captions, the contact highlight, separate volumes, hold or toggle charge, key rebinding) change how strongly a cue is drawn or how input is entered — never the simulation, timing, contact result or replay hash.

## The combat presentation contract (COMBAT-009)

Presentation may **exaggerate** a normalized authoritative value; it may never **invent** one, and it may never feed anything back into the simulation. No presentation code recomputes a combat formula. If a quantity is not on `PresentationSnapshot` or an event payload, the trail and the audio do without it.

The snapshot carries what a swing means, so the renderer never has to re-derive it: `tip_speed`, `launch_preparation`, `swing_progress`, `swing_potential`, `commitment`, `stability`, `facing_error`, `stable_side`, `guard_region`, and `burst`; `exposure` and `threat_time` — two bounded threat predictions a tick — only when the projection is asked for them (`SnapshotProjector.project(..., threat_readings)`, today the debug overlay); the snapshot says whether it carries them (`has_threat_readings`), and without them both hold neutral defaults that MUST NOT be read as measurements. Final strike quality is deliberately absent — it only exists at contact, so it arrives on the `body_hit` payload with its `grade` already named.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

- **Before contact, nothing predicts.** The interface shows the attacker's own potential and lets the target's vulnerability read from their own physical state — displaced weapon, committed swing, bad angle, carried momentum, recovery pose. Convergence is revealed only at contact, so the interface never lies.
- **Progress is travel.** Because `swing_progress` comes from physical travel rather than an animation clock, a blade stopped by another blade visibly stops. Authored animation follows the simulated sword geometry, not the reverse.

## Reading a swing from the primitives

The blade ribbon (`SwordTrail3D`) is the main combat read, and it is a reading rather than decoration. Each sample carries the `swing_potential` the simulation computed at that instant, and the ribbon's silhouette thickness and opacity come from it: thin while the swing is gathering, heaviest through the genuinely dangerous part of the arc, tapering as the swing spends itself. Because the potential is taken from the blade and not from the input that produced it, the heavy part lands in a slightly different place every swing — there is a living acceleration curve to learn, not a crit frame to memorize.

The blade's percussion band is drawn as a **separate, narrower rib floating just above the band**, so the sweet region reads as its own shape rather than as a tint. Colour may reinforce it; it never carries it alone. Its position comes from `SwingSemantics.SWEET_REGION_MIN/MAX` (`0.55`–`0.90` of the hilt-to-tip span), so there is exactly one definition of where that rib is. The rib is that band, not the centre of percussion and not a live leverage reading. Current slash drawing, and what a later effect may add, is [Slash ribbon, sweet region, and leverage](#slash-ribbon-sweet-region-and-leverage).

Wind-back needs no invented cue, because the charge itself is shown: the hold gathers the displayed sword up toward the striking-side shoulder, point rising with the charge actually earned, biased to the side the cut will come from (the high load; see _Authored rigs follow the displayed sword_). Audio tightens behind it: one held voice per fighter whose pitch and level ride the wind-back actually earned. It plateaus at the top because `charge` saturates at the most that is physically reachable, not because of an authored cap. There is no charge glow.

Footwork writes on the floor and swings write in the air, so the two can never be confused at a glance. A burst kicks flat dust at ankle height, drifting _opposite_ the heading the simulation froze and carried on the `burst_started` payload.

A miss is information, not a non-event: the swing follows through into a readable recovery pose and is heard as a hollow follow-through, and nothing punctuates it — no hitstop, no contact sound, no spark.

## Slash ribbon, sweet region, and leverage

Physics stays the source. This section is the current drawing, so a later pass can mark leverage on the slash without inventing a second sword or a damage bonus. Laws: [combat](./combat.md) (sweet spot, lever, efficiency), [COMBAT-009](../../spec/invariants.md#combat-009), [TRAIL-PARITY-001](../../spec/invariants.md#trail-parity-001). Code: `SwingSemantics` (`game/src/domain/combat/swing_semantics.gd`), `ContactMechanics.percussion_fraction` and `WeaponKinematics.lever` (`game/src/domain/combat/`), `SwordTrail3D` (`game/src/presentation/entities/sword_trail_3d.gd`), `FighterPresentation3D.trail_points`.

Three different facts are easy to draw as one:

- **Sweet region.** `SWEET_REGION_MIN` `0.55` to `SWEET_REGION_MAX` `0.90` along the blade (`0` guard, `1` tip). `in_sweet_region` is a diagnostic. The resolver does not read it. Damage reads the continuous efficiency curve.
- **Centre of percussion.** `ContactMechanics.percussion_radius` is `I_grip / (m·a)` about the middle of the two hands. `percussion_fraction` is that point on the blade. The bastard sword's cut-efficiency curve peaks there (`weapon_catalog.gd`: fractions `0`, `0.35`, that fraction, `1`; values `0.35`, `0.75`, `1`, `0.8`). [combat](./combat.md) records the derived fraction as `0.633`. Struck there, the sword turns about the hands. It is not "where the sword hits hardest." Near the guard the contact is heavy (most impulse, least energy). Near the point it is light and fast.
- **Leverage.** `WeaponKinematics.lever` is the weapon Jacobian at a point, projected on a normal: how fast that point moves per unit `θ̇`, and the arm an impulse acts through. Effective mass at that point is `1 / (1/m + c²/M_θ)`. A longer lever is a lighter, faster point. The longest lever on a square cut is toward the tip, which is the weak of the blade, not the place to mark as the best hit.

**What the slash draws today**

- Each trail sample is the authoritative planar hilt and tip, raised to the drawn blade's height (`trail_points`). The ribbon's ground footprint is the space collision uses.
- Thickness is `swing_potential` at that instant: `0` unless the phase is striking; otherwise `(tip speed / full-swing tip speed)²`, times structure (launch preparation × stability, floored at `0.35`). Charge is not multiplied in again. Potential says how dangerous this part of the swing is. It does not say whether it will land.
- The sheet is one thickness from hilt to tip. The hilt vertex is fainter (`0.55`) than the tip (`0.85`). Lifetime is `0.14` s. Half-width runs from `0.006` m to `0.05` m. At most sixteen samples. A trail strength of `0` draws nothing and still ages samples.
- The newest sample is the full leading edge. Older samples narrow toward `SwordTrail3D.TAIL_WIDTH`. Reduced Motion draws only the latest two samples and keeps the rest in the history.
- The sweet rib is on when sweet-spot strength is above `0` and the loudest sample's potential is at least `0.3`. It is the span from `0.55` to `0.90` of every drawn sample, lifted `0.014` m. Its midpoint is `0.725`, which is not `percussion_fraction`.
- The percussion glint is a second strip on the same mesh, one short mark per drawn sample at `ContactMechanics.percussion_fraction` of that sample's hilt-to-tip span. `MatchPresenter` and the set intro pass the weapon's fraction. The drawer does not hardcode `0.633`. The glint is absent until that fraction is set, and absent when the sweet-spot cue is off.
- F3 (`DebugVectors3D`) draws a long cross at the sweet-band midpoint and a shorter cross at `percussion_fraction`. It does not draw `lever`, because leverage needs a contact normal.
- Contact sparks, clang pitch, and camera use the contact that happened. Clash spark count, spark speed, and camera impulse follow `CombatFeedbackDirector.response()` of `INTENSITY`. A carried blade fraction at or below `GUARD_FRACTION` (`0.2`) tightens that burst. Missing fractions are not treated as the guard. Sparks are not placed on the glint.

**What is still not drawn**

`WeaponKinematics.lever` and effective mass are not on the ribbon. The rib does not move when `θ` changes the live lever. A thrust and a slash share this ribbon; a thrust stays narrow only because the hilt and tip do not sweep apart. Do not mark the tip as highest leverage, and do not read `SwingSemantics` back into the solver.

## Impact feedback comes from physical channels

Each feedback channel is driven by the physical quantity that actually causes it, and by nothing else:

| Channel                | Driven by                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hitstop                | clash `intensity`, or a strike's `quality`                 |
| Spark count and speed  | clash `intensity`                                          |
| Spark direction        | the carried contact `normal` and `strike` direction        |
| Recoil streak          | the carried `normal` scaled by the `push` actually applied |
| Camera impulse         | the same intensity or quality, along the same normal       |
| Contact audio category | the resolver's own contact class                           |
| Clang pitch (timbre)   | where the swords met: blade fractions, or a hilt knock     |
| Held scrape loudness   | friction work `\|J_t\|·v_t` at the held point              |
| Grind sparks           | the same friction work, banked into a spark rate           |
| Swing whoosh           | `swing_potential`, so weapon length and mass are in it     |

A clash's `intensity` is the energy the solve took out of it against a reference strong clash from the same mechanics, as an amplitude (`√(E / E_ref)`, `ContactMechanics.reference_clash_energy`): two swords meeting square at their centres of percussion read exactly as their closing speed over `blade_strong_speed`, while a crash at the hilts the bodies take reads harder and a flick off a free point softer — never by closing speed alone, and never by charge.

Hitstop is authored as three **bands** on the kit — `hitstop_blade` (0–40 ms), `hitstop_body` (25–65 ms), and `hitstop_devastating` (60–90 ms) — and interpolated by the physics. There is no value per class name, which is exactly why an awful heavy graze cannot feel stronger than a perfect tap riposte: quality is what the mapping reads. It remains wall-clock only; authoritative time never freezes (HITSTOP-001).

Contact geometry is **carried, not reconstructed**. `blade_collision` and `body_hit` payloads include the collision normal, the direction the striking point was travelling, and for a strike the velocity change the impulse imparted. Presentation previously rebuilt a normal by rotating the vector between the two fighters, which is a guess that can disagree with the impulse that was actually applied; now the sparks, the recoil, and the camera all lean the way the resolver says the blow went.

Swords held against each other **scrape as hard as friction works there** (`ContactScrape`): the held point doing the most friction work this tick, `|J_t|·v_t`, sets the scrape's loudness continuously (as an amplitude) at the shared rendered contact; a blade pressed still does no friction work and is silent, a light slide is faint, a hard drag loud. The scrape is keyed by that point's `contact_id`, so one contact keeps one take for as long as it lasts and never re-triggers, and it lets go the tick no point is held; grind sparks are thrown at a rate set by the same work (banked, so the rate is deterministic). There is no "bind after N ticks" timer, and deliberately **no hitstop**: a held contact is not a hit. With particles off the scrape is still heard.

The camera reinforces boundaries only — nothing for an ordinary swing, barely anything for a clash, and even a devastating strike stays inside a restrained ceiling. This is a duel read in millimetres.

Victim reaction comes from the simulation first. The proxy stands exactly where the state says even on the frame it is struck; the flinch is animation, and there is no recoil overlay that could throw a target further than the impulse did.

Accessibility scales how loudly these cues are drawn and never what they mean. `trail_strength` may remove the ribbon entirely or thicken it; `sweet_spot_cue` may turn the rib off or exaggerate it. Neither moves the sweet region, changes damage, or changes timing. Both come from `PlayerSettings`, so `Off` is a supported way to play rather than a handicap: with shake, motion, the ribbon, and the rib all switched off, a hit is still marked in the world, still heard, still felt in the pacing (proven by PRES-PRESENTER).

`PlayerSettings` also provides continuous `screen_shake` (0.0–1.0), `flash_intensity`, `particle_intensity`, and a `combat_readability` mode (NORMAL / ENHANCED). Legacy `bool` screen_shake migrates on load (`true` → 1.0, `false` → 0.0). `reduced_motion` remains as a convenience preset that lowers motion-related settings together. None of these reach the simulation — a settings-hash-invariance test proves that different presentation settings produce identical authoritative hashes.

## Combat readability state

`PresentationFighter` carries normalized 0-1 readability scalars computed once per tick by `SnapshotProjector`. Raw quantities that are already 0-1 (charge, commitment, exposure when projected, swing_progress, swing_potential, stability) are not duplicated; additional derived readings are:

| Field                  | Source                                  | Description                |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| `recovery_remaining01` | recovery_left / recovery_ticks          | 1 = just started, 0 = done |
| `point_threat01`       | Tip axial closing (PHYS-007 kinematics) | 0 = safe, 1 = lethal line  |
| `movement_speed01`     | speed / max_speed                       | 0 = still, 1 = sprinting   |
| `burst01`              | Gesture burst kind                      | 0 or 1 (burst in progress) |

`ImpactFeedback` (`game/src/presentation/snapshot/impact_feedback.gd`) is a typed read-only projection of contact event payloads, so presentation never reaches into raw dictionaries. It carries normalized impulse/severity, contact geometry, and attacker/target slots.

`DebugVectors3D` draws the quantities themselves — body velocity, the tip's actual direction of travel, and the percussion band — as world-space lines from snapshot values, next to the numbers in the text overlay. One F3 shows both, because a closing speed you cannot see the direction of is half a reading.

## Change art, animation, or sound in one place (PRES-KIT-001)

Each combatant identity has a `CombatantPresentationKit` composing a `FighterPresentationKit` and a `WeaponPresentationKit` (`game/src/presentation/kit/`). This split exists because the same fighter may hold different weapons and different fighters may hold the same weapon.

To replace the primitive look of an identity (e.g. a Blender model):

1. Import the `.glb` and wrap it in a scene (optional `AnimationPlayer` with clips). The pipeline is [docs/reference/assets.md](../reference/assets.md).
2. Author a kit `.tres` at the identity's path in `RiposteKits.AUTHORED_KIT_PATHS` (`game/content/presentation/riposte_kits.gd`) with the scene, animation clips (semantic → clip), and audio cues filled in.
3. Nothing else changes. Authored kits load first; the factory primitive kit fills any gap — a whole identity not yet authored, or any cue or intro an authored kit leaves out, so a half-authored identity is never silent. Debug builds fail closed on a missing kit; release builds use a placeholder.

Generic presentation code never names an identity (lint bans it); kits are resolved once per match (`DuelKits.resolve`).

Alive eyes, the brow bar, and dead-face X marks are all authored on the character (`face` on each part). `FacePresentation.bind` indexes `Eye*`, `Brow*`, and `DeadEye*` once when the proxy is built (`game/src/presentation/entities/face_presentation.gd`). A later pose writes visibility only when `terminal_cause` changes: living eyes while it is `NONE`, the X for a kill or a ring-out. The brow stays visible in both states. DeadEye rotation is around Blender Y so the X sits in the face plane at the eyes, not as a ground-plane slash on the brow. A rematch builds a new proxy, so the living face returns. The set horn is `SetWinningHorn`: the arena kit's `match.horn`, once, when the set winner's opponent is `KILLED`. The sting is one blast stretched to `AudioDirector.HORN_SECONDS` (6 s) by slowing that single play, and the match screen stays up so leaving does not cut it off. It is not started again and it is not a second music loop. It is the stage's horn, not a fighter cue. The showcase plays that fighter's `character_call` under the announcer and does not play the horn. The set intro plays that same call under each fighter's name. `CrowdDirector` names at most one reaction per tick from existing events and the set score; it does not write combat state (`game/src/presentation/audio/crowd_director.gd`). Priority is set win, then `FIGHTER_KILLED` or `RING_OUT`, then a critical, then a clean parry (`DuelEventKeys.MARGIN` at the high end of `CombatFeedbackDirector.PARRY_QUALITY_CEIL`), then an earned set point (a positive score of `rounds_to_win - 1`, on arrival). Lower cues on that tick are dropped, not queued. `ROUND_ENDED` never selects a cue. Cooldown expiry does not play anything. Steel contact, a body hit without a critical, and a deflection do not shoot. Intensity is a separate meter: a meaningful class raises it even when the shot is held, a contiguous run adds once, and decay emits nothing. A suppressed reaction is not replayed when the cooldown expires. First-to-one has no set point, because the opening score is already zero. A finish punches through cheer cooldown once per scoreline; a duplicated kill or ring-out at those scores is silent. The set-win reaction plays once; later ticks with the winner still set are silence so intensity can decay. Cue names are `CrowdDirector.CUE_*`; `MatchPresenter` maps them to `PresentationKit.CUE_CROWD_*` and rides the crowd-bed trim. Sample selection is a second step: `AudioDirector.play_crowd_cue` increments a per-cue key so a second eligible shot does not immediately repeat when the kit has more than one take. One authored take is still that one take. A tick with no class step sets excitement to the max of the decayed value and one level: a body hit at or above `CrowdDirector.HEAVY_SEVERITY` (a graze is zero; a lighter wound does not lift), blade `INTENSITY` only for a strong clash, or a committed `ATTACK_WHIFFED`. Ordinary clashes do not lift the bed. A class step does not add that level again. A committed whiff inside strike reach may play `CUE_CROWD_GASP` after `CrowdDirector.ORDINARY_COOLDOWN_TICKS`; one outside reach only lifts the bed. That wait does not block a set win, and the meter can still rise during it. `AudioDirector` (the crowd bed and one shot) is a child of `MatchPresenter`; `detach_and_dispose` stops it. A rematch is a new screen and a new director, so momentum does not leak. A set-win reaction uses the winner's `crowd_chant` when the kit has one, and the generic victory cheer otherwise. An unknown cue name does not replace the shot. `RiposteApp` owns the one music loop (`game/src/application/app/riposte_app.gd`): the arena theme from match start through Results and the victory showcase, the menu theme on a menu screen. A handoff mutes and stops the current voice, then starts the next stream on the following frame. A missing arena stream stops the loop and clears it. Same stream already playing does not call `play()`. The fighter HUD shows stamina bars (`RiposteTheme.METER_LIGHT` / `METER_DARK`) and the condition icon. It does not show a health bar.

The authoritative weapon angle drives the blade visual. Character animation visually supports the sword position, never the reverse. See [docs/architecture/presentation-feedback.md](../architecture/presentation-feedback.md) for the full architecture.

### Composition layers: skins, voice, intro, match loadout

Cosmetic and match-wide layers sit on top of the identity kits, and every one is optional — `null` means "the base kit's look":

| Layer                              | Holds                                                                     | Resolved                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| `FighterSkinKit` / `WeaponSkinKit` | a replacement scene, or node → mesh (`null` hides) and material overrides | per slot, by skin id, from the catalog       |
| `FighterVoiceKit`                  | the announcer's reading of the name, effort and hurt takes                | on the fighter kit                           |
| `FighterIntroProfile`              | card text, spoken line and caption, the flourish clip                     | on the fighter kit                           |
| `MatchPresentationLoadout`         | arena kit, `AnnouncerKit`, `UiThemeKit`, `VfxStyleKit`                    | once per match (`RiposteKits.match_loadout`) |

`CombatantPresentationKit.of(fighter, weapon, skin, blade_skin)` refuses a skin authored for a different base kit, and an unknown skin id is the base look. Skins are chosen _after_ the rules are fixed (only the local player's slot wears the chosen one) and nothing in them can reach a definition, so reach, mass, and the state hash cannot change with a skin (PRES-RIG proves it with the Training Gear skin).

## Force-scaled feedback

There are no light/medium/heavy buckets. Every channel of a body strike reads one continuous `CombatFeedbackDirector.response()` of the resolver's own quality — flat at the bottom so a graze is barely there, full at the top of an ordinary strike, still growing into the devastating range — so there is no step anywhere along the ladder (PRES-FEEDBACK sweeps it). Contact _kind_ picks the shape (a slash fans, a poke or thrust draws an axial line); it never picks the size. Light and heavy body audio crossfade by damage rather than switching at a threshold.

A lethal blow adds the exceptional stack: the signature accent layer, a lethal flash, one longer freeze, and presentation-only slow motion (`SlowMotionRequest` → `FixedTickDriver.slow_motion`). Like hitstop, slow motion changes only _when_ ticks run, never what they compute (HITSTOP-001). A bind holds its grind and grind sparks for as long as the snapshot says the blades are pinned. `particle_intensity` 0 drops every particle while flash, streak, sound, and pacing still mark the hit.

A **killing blow** produces a `DeathPresentationRequest` — who died, the contact family (a slash cuts down, a thrust runs through), the direction the blow travelled, how hard it landed, and a deterministic key. The lock is the kill, not the stab: the director emits it **only on the blow that leaves the target not alive** (read from the post-tick snapshot), so a non-lethal poke or shallow thrust lands its contact feel and nothing more.

The request goes to a `DeathPresentationController`, which hands it — once per fighter per round — to the first **backend** that can carry it out. Today that is `PrimitiveDeathBackend`. On a killing **thrust** striker and victim play **one shared timeline** (`StabFinisherProfile`, PRES-STAB-001): IMPALE → HOLD → WITHDRAW → BLADE CLEAR → VICTIM COLLAPSE → WEAPON RELEASE. The striker drives in, holds the blade in, and draws it back along the thrust axis (`run_through`, keyed to the same beats); the victim holds impaled on supporting legs — a beat to look down at the blade — and only once the blade is clear do the knees go (`death_stab`; the procedural collapse waits out the same hold), and the victim's own sword drops as the collapse takes the hands. Because it is reached only from a death request, a thrust that does not kill can never lock the striker; a killing cut leaves the striker to follow through on their own swing. The victim's collapse is chosen from the kit's `DeathPresentationProfile`. An authored fighter maps styles to clips (`style_clips`), falling back to its generic `DEATH` clip and then to the procedural collapse; the Wolf falls backward onto his back for a cut (`death`) and folds over the blade, drops to his knees, and pitches forward face-down for a run-through (`death_stab`), while the striker holds the driven lunge (`finisher_clip` → `run_through`) rather than the dash, which steps back to guard on its own. On the primitive body the same styles are procedural: a cut drops it backward, a stab pitches it forward, folding styles sink it. A one-shot clip holds its last frame for as long as its state lasts — the proxy starts a clip when the clip changes, never because the last one ended — so a dead body stays down. A **ragdoll** is a future optional backend placed ahead of the primitive, not a replacement: it is added only once the rig's `Skeleton3D` has a generated, trimmed `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D` and web/mobile profiling shows it earns its cost — until then the controller falls through to the primitive. Whatever backend runs, it lives on a presentation-only collision layer, starts from the current rendered pose rather than bind pose, and never reports back, so it cannot change a hit, a ring-out, the winner, or the replay hash. Unit tests cover the request and the orchestration (one start per death, fall-through when a backend is unavailable) against a fake backend; actual ragdoll motion is a device-QA pass, never a unit assertion.

A **ring-out** is not a death and is never an immediate ragdoll. The simulation ends the round the tick a fighter crosses the edge, so authority stops moving them at the lip; the director then emits a `FallPresentationRequest` carrying the exact exit position and the horizontal momentum carried over the edge, and the proxy's fall **owns the root trajectory** from there (`root_at`: exit momentum carried forward unchanged, presentation gravity drawing the body down). A fighter shoved hard flies out; one who stepped off drops near the edge — the fall reads the knockback that caused it rather than dropping straight down at the lip. A raw ragdoll started at the ledge could flop backward or snag and visually deny that knockback, so the root always follows this trajectory; a ragdoll may later own only the limbs. The body itself plays the `RING_FALL` semantic — one of the Wolf's authored flails (`ring_fall_a/b/c`), chosen from the fall's event key through `DeathPresentationProfile.ring_fall_clips`, so a replay falls the same way — and an arm is thrown above its shoulder within a quarter second.

**One terminal presentation per fighter, from authority** (PRES-TERMINAL-001). A fighter can be marked over the edge and killed in the same tick, so the director may emit both a death and a fall for them. Presentation never settles that by which request came first: the snapshot carries each fighter's `terminal_cause` (the simulation's own chronological reading, COMBAT-014), and `TerminalPresentationPolicy` keeps only the requests that agree — `KILLED` plays the death and its release, `RING_OUT` plays the fall and the airborne release, and a tick where both fighters are out presents both. Every terminal latch (death, finisher, fall, weapon drop, grip, the one-shot clip) clears at the next round. **A terminal hands the body to its clip.** From the moment a terminal is presented — a ring fall at the ledge, a death when its collapse starts (a run-through's victim stays at full pose while still standing on the blade) — the sword pose (`CombatPoseModifier3D`: chest, spine and hips following the sword) fades from 1 to 0 over a deterministic 0.10–0.22 s window from the event key; the grip IK holds the hands on the sword only while it is still held and leaves it over the same window once it is released, so a falling or collapsing body is never a guard pose painted over a flail. `TerminalPresentationKind` names what a body presents — `NONE`, `SLASH_DEATH`, `STAB_DEATH`, `RING_FALL`, `FALLEN_BODY` (the collapse finished), `FALLING_CORPSE` — read from authority's alive/falling facts and the latched death style, never deciding anything. **A body is drawn where authority has it** (FALLEN-BODY-001). Once a killed fighter lies, the proxy draws it on the snapshot's body axis (`body_yaw`) through the authoritative centre of mass as the winner pushes and turns it; the axis is symmetric, so of `yaw` and `yaw + π` it takes the nearer and the death clip's head stays the head — a push never flips the body. Being moved, it reads loose: a small roll about its long axis scaled continuously by its surface speed (centre speed plus spin at its ends), still at rest. Carried over the edge (`body_over_edge`) it drops under presentation gravity from wherever authority carries it outward and flails one of the authored falls, chosen by its key (`FALLING_CORPSE`).

**Terminal choreography runs on presentation-scaled time** (PRES-TIME-001). Fighter clips play on Godot's normal process step — so the skeleton's modifiers (body support, IK) run after the animation as the engine orders them — at a `speed_scale` of presentation-scaled ÷ real time (0 in hitstop); the death collapse, the finisher hold, the fall trajectory, the sword-release timers, and the grip ease-off take `FixedTickDriver.presentation_delta()` directly — so a death freezes through the lethal hitstop and collapses through the slow motion instead of finishing ahead of the duel it belongs to. Camera, VFX, trails, audio, and UI stay on real time; dropped swords are Godot physics.

A fighter out of the round **drops their sword**. On a kill, the release is part of the death style the kit resolves: `DeathPresentationProfile.resolve` returns the style, its clip, and its `DeathStyleTiming` together, and the presenter queues the drop at that style's `weapon_release_time` (the Wolf's backward cut-down lets go as the collapse begins; a run-through's release comes from the shared stab timeline, after the blade is clear and the collapse has started). Over the edge the hands let go a deterministic beat after the ledge (0.12–0.18 s from the event key), which is not tied to an authored clip and so stays on `WeaponDropRequest`. A survived hit never drops it. When the hands let go, the held look is hidden — the simulation-posed `WeaponOrigin` itself never moves — and a `DroppedWeapon3D` rigid body carries a fresh look built from the same kits (`CombatantVisuals`: weapon scene, weapon skin, the side's colorway), never a clone of the live subtree. It weighs the authored weapon's mass (carried on the snapshot as `weapon_mass`), is placed and then has its physics interpolation reset before it moves, and launches with the blade's own spin from the authoritative blade angle and speed plus the presented body's motion at that instant (still for a collapse in place, the fall trajectory over the edge), so the exit momentum is never counted twice. Its tumble is **physics-consistent from the captured launch state** for a simplified collider — one thin slab over the blade, with the engine's centre of mass at the slab's middle rather than near the guard — so collider, centre of mass, friction, bounce, and angular damping are device-QA tuning knobs, not claims of physical accuracy. The arm IK eases off the empty grip, and the next round clears the floor and puts the sword back in hand. Presentation physics uses its own layers (`PresentationPhysicsLayers`): the sword collides only with the presentation floor (the platform top at the rules' radius, on either arena path), so it lands inside the edge and falls past it, and nothing authoritative is touched — Riposte's combat uses no Godot physics at all. When and how the sword drops is unit-tested; its tumble is device QA.

A **parry** is given a physical deflection read whose brightness is the beat margin (`DuelEventKeys.MARGIN`, how far ahead the defender became threatening): a decisive deflection flashes a tighter ring, throws a hotter spark fan, rings a crisper and higher clash cue, and snaps the camera harder than a bare-threshold one. Riposte has no parry button and no parry bonus, so this feedback **borrows none of a hit's weight** — no hitstop, no slow motion, no damage, no stun. It only tells the eye how cleanly the simulation's deflection landed.

## Authored audio

A cue may hold one stream or an array of takes. The take for an event is chosen from `CombatFeedbackDirector.variant_key` (tick, actor, target), so a replay hears the same takes in the same order. `audio_layers` name cues that sound with a primary from the same event (a strong clash is transient + metal tail + low impact). Buses: Master → Music, SFX (→ Combat, UI), Voice; announcer volume and captions are player settings. Every voiced line requests a caption, even when the line is missing or muted.

## Set introduction

A set is one match — first to `rounds_to_win` rounds — and the application opens a new `MatchSet` (with its own `set_id`) for every Quick Play and every Rematch. Each set opens with `SetIntroDirector` (~2.3 s), claimed once through `claim_set_intro`: the first fighter's close-up and card, a versus beat, the second fighter, then a cut back to the gameplay camera under the sting, with the fighters in their starting guards.

**Every round then starts with "MATCH N … FIGHT!"** (`RoundStartDirector`, PRES-ROUND-START-001). The round's own intro ticks (1.2 s) are the window: "MATCH N" over the set score — the same sword-point pips, each side named — as the round is introduced, and "FIGHT!" on the very tick it goes live, landing large and settling. Both are called in the intro announcer's voice from pre-generated takes and captioned. A press held through the card is discarded at the transition; the first live tick takes input with no grace period. The gameplay HUD — plates, bars, pips, clock, Pause — appears with "FIGHT!": one policy in `MatchScreen` (PRES-CHROME-001) shows it only while a duel is being fought, never over the set intro, the round card, or the set's final beat and victory, and no director toggles it. Presentation says "Match"; the domain keeps "round". New rounds inside the match are never introduced; a Rematch is a new set and is introduced again. Training is practice, not a set, and has neither an introduction nor a victory. The clock is stopped for the whole intro, so the first authoritative tick is identical with or without it; any deliberate press skips it and never becomes an attack. The showcase figures are display-only (`FighterShowcaseActor`, shared with the set victory): their swords perform an authored flourish, because no collision exists during the shot. What the announcer reads is content (`FighterIntroProfile.spoken_name`): a fighter is read by one name from either slot, so in a mirror match both are "Wolf."

## Set victory

When a set resolves with a winner, the winner is shown once (`claim_set_victory`, PRES-VICTORY-001). At the deciding round's end the snapshot's `set_winner` — the simulation's own `MatchOutcome` rule — tells `MatchScreen` the set is decided; the deciding round then plays out **live** as its final beat: the post-round freedom and the result run at the real clock with the round's winner still in control (`DuelSimulation.accepts_gameplay_input`), the gameplay HUD hidden, and the winner's showcase follows the match's own end. Nothing is fast-forwarded or hidden, and every terminal's mandatory beat fits that window (PRES-PRESENTER). A draw, a no-contest, and Training get the plain results.

`ResultsScreen` then opens on the showcase. A `VictoryDirector` builds its own dark stage in the presentation mount and a fresh `FighterShowcaseActor` from the winner's resolved combatant kit — fighter, skin, weapon, weapon skin, and the colorway for their side — never a match proxy, which is already gone. The director plays the profile's (`FighterVictoryProfile`) authored cinematic on real time, through the duel's own carriage landmarks: from the mid guard a quick cut right → left, the quick return cut, then the gather into the right high shoulder load — and **hold**. There is no third, released attack. The stage darkens through the gather and stays dark; the last stretch of the return cut's streak, projected to screen space, becomes the gold divider beside the name on the `VictoryCard`; the fighter's howl lands on the charge lock; the camera pushes from a full-body 3/4 into the profile while the load is held; the card reads **WOLF — VICTOR**; the announcer reads "Wolf wins." (correctly, this time), captioned. The results sheet is hidden and its buttons disabled until the cinematic ends (~2.2 s) or a deliberate press skips it — and that press never activates a button. Wolf then holds `victory_idle`, a subtle breathing version of the held load. Leaving frees the showcase and silences it.

The showcase actor is the intro's too. `FighterShowcaseActor` builds through `CombatantVisuals` (so the intro now wears the resolved mirror colorway as well), runs a manual `AnimationPlayer` advanced by its director, and carries a display sword. While the playing clip keys the rig's unweighted `SwordMount` bone, the sword rides that bone's animated pose — true pitch, overhead guards, diagonal cuts the gameplay blade may never show — and the hands follow it through the same two-hand IK; otherwise the sword sits at hand height and the director may turn it.

## Authored rigs follow the displayed sword

The combat sword is one rigid blade on one horizontal plane: yaw θ from the combat heading, its hands on the stretched orbit `C(θ)` (WEAPON-PATH-003). That orbit is already a pose Wolf can hold — the whole sword clears his torso and the near arm keeps its reach — so presentation draws exactly that sword and only lifts it (PRES-CARRIAGE-001, WEAPON-ORBIT-005/006):

```text
interpolated θ → WeaponKinematics            (carriage C(θ), yaw θ — the collision sword)
  → CombatPresentationField + CutPresentationCurve   (height, pitch, roll about the carriage)
  → displayed sword (the SwordCarriage node)         (same carriage, same yaw, lifted)
  → grip markers + ElbowGuideField poles             (hands on the sword, elbows' bend planes)
  → CombatPoseModifier3D → TwoBoneIK3D             (body support, head on the target, then both arms)
```

The presenter interpolates θ between ticks and evaluates the orbit there, so every drawn pose lies on it; it never blends transforms. The lift's continuous inputs — swing progress and charge — are interpolated with θ (`MatchPresenter.lift_inputs`), so drawn between ticks (a faster display, slow motion) the lift moves with the sword instead of stepping once a tick; across a phase change, or when a cut commits into the opposite side, the lift's branch changes and the proxy's carry blend inertializes the difference. PRES-RIG proves it by adaptive refinement: a real cut is redrawn at 8 and 32 sub-steps wherever it turns more than 1° or changes phase, and the worst excess of the drawn point over the authoritative one must shrink — a step keeps its size, motion does not. That makes the presenter the one owner of interpolation: Godot's automatic physics interpolation stays off for the fighter proxies (it is off project-wide), because interpolating the transform `C(θ)` produced would interpolate the same motion twice and draw chords off the orbit. If it is ever turned on for anything else, the proxies opt out. `WeaponOrigin` is the authoritative planar sword (the domain's pommel on the blade plane, yaw θ, never drawn); the steel, the x-ray ghost and the grip targets hang from `SwordCarriage`, which shares its carriage and yaw and adds only the lift — so the hands, the displayed sword and collision are one rigid object. Pitch foreshortens the drawn blade's ground projection (about 2 cm at the tip through the near-level contact corridor); nothing re-aims it. There is no horizontal offset, yaw correction, tip targeting or side-specific shove anywhere in presentation. Trails follow the displayed tip; sparks come from authoritative contact coordinates.

The hands' track is the hilt track of the simulation itself: the original radial weapon origin (fighter centre + θ + a straight sword), projected out to where two hands hold the sword. Wolf's rig poses are evidence for where its four landmarks sit, measured offline and symmetrized; they never drive it at runtime. Everything the body does to hold that sword is `BodySupportPose`, sampled once per frame from the same interpolated θ as the hilt: torso coil effort and its per-side cap, the elbow guides' inputs, gather, and the lift. Two projections of one scalar — the hilt track for physics, `BodySupportPose` for the human — so the body cannot drift from the sword. Support is continuous in θ: the guard bands below are readings with hysteresis, and crossing one never steps the torso. The weapon is an exact mirror on both sides; the right-lead body is not, and supports each side with its own (deterministic) coil cap while the legs keep the same stance at ±135° as at 0°.

### The guard field: Riposte names, HEMA-informed

The simulation has one freely swung blade and no discrete guard. Presentation reads its angle (negative is the fighter's right; the canonical guard is −45°) as a continuous field, with `GuardPoseField` supplying the discrete side and band and the hysteresis (enter Longpoint inside 12°, leave past 18°). Where the hands are on the plane is the orbit's at every one of these; the names below say how the sword is lifted and how the body supports it:

```text
RIGHT_LOAD — RIGHT_READY — LONGPOINT — LEFT_READY — LEFT_LOAD
```

- **MID_GUARD** (READY, around ±45°) is Riposte's point-threatening neutral: both hands well forward of the abdomen at navel to low-sternum height, the point forward and about 12° raised toward the opponent's opening on that side. It draws on Langort, Pflug, and Ochs without claiming to be any one of them. It is _not_ Meyer's Mittelhut, which carries the sword near horizontal at the waist with the point back.
- **LONGPOINT** is the centreline and the thrust corridor, and the longest posture in the field: hands farthest forward, elbows least flexed, the point at the opponent, the pommel well clear of the abdomen, the torso still bladed rather than squared.
- **HIGH_LOAD** is a full charge. The hold gathers the sword up toward the striking-side shoulder, where the hands are beside that shoulder and forward of the torso and the point is high (about 65°), inspired by Vom Tag at the shoulder and Zornhut. Charge reads mostly as point height, and the load stays biased to its side so the opponent can see where the cut will come from. The planar wind-back to ±135° is the stored state, not where the hands go.
- **CUT** (`CutPresentationCurve`) is keyed by the cut angle (the authoritative angle along the launch direction), never by animation time. The loaded sword descends as the blade travels, comes near horizontal through centre, carries to about −15° just past the opposite guard, and then, once the blade is committed into that side (angle past 45° with the angular velocity still travelling there; it lets go only back inside 30°), the **pitch inflection** rises it into the opposite guard. A right→left and a left→right release trace the crossing diagonals of Riposte's X. A blade stopped, deflected, or reversed by contact stops, deflects, or reverses the picture with it.

Every function is continuous within a phase. Where the field answers from a different branch across a phase boundary, the proxy inertializes the jump in height, pitch and roll: it keeps the difference as an offset decaying over 60 ms of presentation time, so the drawn sword never pops while the field stays pure — and never leaves the orbit horizontally.

### A bladed hand-and-a-half fencer

Wolf stands in the **Riposte Two-Handed Mid Guard**: hand-and-a-half sword leverage and longsword landmarks (Longpoint, the shoulder loads) on a stable, kendo-like right-foot-forward base — a deliberate hybrid, not a historical HEMA or kendo stance (PRES-STANCE-001). Historical German longsword often changes the lead with the side of the guard; Riposte does not — the silhouette stays readable, locomotion never swaps feet, and an attack can reverse as fast as its physics does; same-lead gathering steps are the HEMA basis. Simulation facing stays opponent-forward and owns movement, the ±135° sword angle, and every contact; `FighterStanceProfile` turns only the presented body: the whole rig by the pelvis's blade (12°), the spine and chest to a 22.5° shoulder line — semi-bladed, neither square nor a fencer's profile —, the head counter-rotated back onto the opponent. The feet stand a real base apart — about 24 cm across the line, authored as a small thigh opening in every grounded clip with the soles rolled back level, not pushed outward at runtime (a runtime hip offset drags the planted feet). In a settled guard the sword may coil the chest only ±2.5°, so the torso keeps a 20–25° blade wherever the sword rests instead of squaring up behind it; a charge or a cut coils further on top of that base. The feet carry the blade too (STANCE-FOOT-001): after the clip, each foot turns about the vertical at its ankle — the lead toe 8° off the combat heading, mostly into the fight, the rear foot opened to 25° — so the clip's foot placement and width are untouched, a planted foot never skates, and no gait clip can square the feet. During commitment the head keeps the opponent while the lead foot, hips and sword stay on the committed line, so a whiff reads as one. Both hands hold the handle the way a hand-and-a-half sword is held (GRIP-001): the right hand just under the guard, the left just above the pommel, fixed points on the sword that never swap or slide — the arms reorganize around them. The sword angle is never re-measured against the bladed chest. Held loads coil per side on top — 10° toward the lead (right) side, 14° toward the rear (left) — and the released cut gets the fuller chain (35° cap), so charge reads as point height and gathered arms, not a body chasing the raw angle. A walking **poke** keeps the Longpoint carriage; a committed **thrust** gets the dash's body support.

### Elbows are structure

Each elbow is a first-class output (PRES-ARMS-001). `ElbowGuideField` derives one guide per arm from that arm's displayed hand in the bladed torso frame — behind it, outward, a little below, by the landmark in `ArmStructureProfile` (mid guard moderately flexed and wide; Longpoint less flexed and less flared; the high load's attacking elbow folded and open outward, its support elbow forward of the ribs; follow-through as the roles exchange) — with Longpoint as the bridge between sides and the roles switching on the blade's own inflection. The pole is the shoulder-side horizontal projected off the shoulder→hand line, so the bend sign never has to flip, plus a downward hang that fades in only as that projection shrinks — renormalizing a vanishing projection is what folded the right elbow on a released cut. The support arm takes a little extra forward while a load is gathered, which keeps its forearm off the ribs. PRES-RIG proves through the guard field, both charge gathers, and both full-charge releases at 1° that no elbow bends toward the torso, none jumps beyond a per-degree budget, and IK never stretches an arm. F3 draws the same solve: shoulder, elbow, hand, the guide, the pole, and the clearance proxies.

### Nothing passes through the fighter

A pose is valid only if the whole rigid chain reaches its grips without penetrating the fighter (PRES-POSE-001). `SelfClearanceProfile` holds Wolf's primitive clearance proxies — the chest, lower gambeson, and belt as elliptic prisms carried by their bones, the head as a sphere — and the gaps poses must keep; `PoseClearance` measures them deterministically, with no physics bodies. Nothing at runtime moves the sword to clear the body: the domain proves the rigid sword fits round the fighter, and PRES-RIG sweeps guard, charge, cut, and recovery to prove the arms can hold it — no hand, wrist, forearm, elbow, pommel, or blade penetrates. A pose that fails is fixed in the body (stance, coil, elbow guidance), in the lift, or — as a rules change — in the orbit, never with a presentation offset. The visible-air margins above that (2 cm hands and forearms, 3 cm elbows, 2 cm weapon) are tuning targets the suite reports.

## Rules of thumb

- Proxies are passive: only `MatchPresenter.render` poses them.
- Feedback scales with physics (contact class, damage) and honors `PresentationOptions` (reduced motion disables camera impulses; reduced flash dims effects; hitstop still conveys impact). Per-identity feel (hitstop, trails) lives in kits; generic feedback constants (spark counts, ring sizes, swing mix, impulse ceilings, audio tiers) live on `CombatFeedbackDirector` and are referenced by `MatchPresenter`. VFX-specific constants (sizes, lifetimes, spreads) live on `VfxDirector`.
- Styling goes through `RiposteTheme`: components pick a `theme_type_variation` (`HudPlate`, `HudStack`, `HudRow`, `HudTitleLabel`, `HudAccentLabel`, `METER_LIGHT`, `METER_DARK`, …). The only per-node style is runtime safe-area margins (`RiposteTheme.apply_insets`).
- Blades render without depth test so the sword is always readable (combat-critical information).
- Icons are drawn shapes (`HudIcons`, `PipRow`), never glyphs the shipped font lacks.
- Audio: combat cues on the Combat bus, round stings on SFX, menu cues on UI, the announcer (`VoiceAnnouncer`) and `character_call` (`VoiceFighter`) on two Voice players so a victory howl does not replace the announcer; the announcer player is 6 dB above unity and the howl player is 20 dB below unity, so the spoken line leads the victory call, crowd bed, one-shot reactions, and the name (`CrowdChant`, only when the stream is already a loop) on Crowd. `RiposteApp` plays the one Music-bus loop as a stream, not a sample: the arena kit's `music` cue from the moment the match screen publishes it, through Results and the victory showcase, and `MENU_LOOP` on a menu screen (`game/src/application/app/riposte_app.gd`). A handoff stops the previous voice and starts the next one on the following frame, so an MP3's stacked playback cannot leave the menu theme in the mix with the arena theme. A missing arena stream stops that loop. Automated tests prove the selected stream, playing state, and that `menu_theme.mp3` is not still playing on another player; they do not prove what the speakers played. The crowd bed's `volume_db` is a presentation trim; `PlayerSettings.crowd_volume` still scales the Crowd bus.
